# Episode Structure

This is a repeatable framework, not a rigid script. Every section below can flex in length and order based on what's actually happening with the guest — see the Interviewer Rule in `02_INTERVIEW_FRAMEWORK.md`. Target long-form length: **approximately 25–40 minutes.**

## A. Cold Open (30–60 seconds)

Use the strongest moment from the episode, pulled forward. It should create curiosity, not summarize the episode.

Examples:
- "90% of people would replace this part even though it's perfectly fine."
- "A BMW came in with 15 faults. The actual problem was something nobody expected."

Full guidance: `05_COLD_OPEN.md`.

## B. Fast Intro (30–90 seconds)

Introduce, quickly: The Link, the guest, the location, and why this person is interesting. Avoid long, formal introductions — get to the guest.

## C. Who Is This Person?

Let the guest tell their story. Starting question: "Who are you, and what made you specialize in this?" Do not over-script — if the guest is telling a great story, follow it (see Interviewer Rule).

## D. The Place

Move through the actual location. Show tools, cars/objects, equipment, work in progress, people, small details, unusual things. The environment is part of the episode, not a backdrop.

## E. Expertise / Value

The educational core. Cover common problems, common mistakes, misconceptions, expensive mistakes, diagnostic issues, things customers misunderstand, warning signs, maintenance advice, specialist-only knowledge. Draw from `03_QUESTION_BANK.md`, Expertise and Educational categories.

## F. Real Stories

Find unusual cases, funny situations, difficult jobs, unexpected solutions, memorable customers, mistakes, failures, successes. This is where the episode gets its personality.

## G. Practical Demonstration

Whenever possible: "Show us." Use a real car/object/tool/problem when appropriate and permitted. The audience should see expertise happening, not just hear about it.

## H. Human / Light Moment

Give the guest room to be funny, personal, or spontaneous. Comedy should come naturally — never forced.

## I. Final Value

End with one highly useful takeaway. Example: "If you own a BMW, what's one thing you wish every owner knew?"

## J. Community Question

Ask the audience a question designed to generate comments, not just "like and subscribe." Examples: "What's the worst problem you've ever had with your car?" "What specialist should we visit next?" "What should we ask this expert in a future live?" See `07_COMMUNITY/02_COMMENT_STRATEGY.md`.

## Notes on Using This Structure

- This is a map, not a script order that must be filmed sequentially. Location (D) and demonstration (G) will often happen naturally intertwined with expertise (E) and stories (F) as the crew walks the space.
- Every section is a candidate source for short-form extraction — see `02_CONTENT_STRATEGY/05_CONTENT_REPURPOSING.md` and `04_HIGHLIGHT_SYSTEM.md`.
- The cold open (A) is built last, after the shoot, once the actual strongest moment is known — see `05_COLD_OPEN.md`.
