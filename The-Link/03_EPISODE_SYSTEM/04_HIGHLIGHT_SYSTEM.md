# Highlight System

## The Principle

Every long-form recording should be treated as a content mine. Highlights are identified *during* filming, not only discovered later in the edit — this is what makes `02_CONTENT_STRATEGY/05_CONTENT_REPURPOSING.md` actually work at production speed.

## Highlight Categories to Watch For During Filming

- **Educational highlights** — a clear, standalone piece of useful knowledge.
- **Surprising highlights** — something the audience almost certainly didn't expect.
- **Funny highlights** — genuine, unforced humor.
- **Emotional highlights** — a moment of real feeling, pride, frustration, or connection.
- **Controversial-but-responsible highlights** — a strong, defensible opinion that will generate discussion without being reckless or misleading.
- **Practical tips** — advice a viewer could act on immediately.
- **Expert statements** — a single, quotable, authoritative line.
- **"You won't believe this" moments** — a story beat with a strong twist.
- **Myth/fact moments** — a common belief directly confirmed or debunked.
- **Customer mistakes** — a recurring error real customers make.
- **Expensive mistakes** — a mistake with a clear, relatable cost.
- **Unusual cases** — a story that stands out even among other stories from the same guest.

## Field Workflow

1. **During filming**, a crew member (ideally not the interviewer, who should stay focused on the guest) logs a rough timestamp and one-line tag whenever a moment matching a category above happens.
2. **Immediately after filming**, the team reviews the highlight log together while memory is fresh, adding anything missed.
3. **In post**, editors start short-form extraction from the highlight log first, then do a full pass of the raw footage as a safety net — not the reverse. This keeps short-form turnaround fast (see `06_POST_PRODUCTION/02_SHORT_FORM_EDITING.md`).
