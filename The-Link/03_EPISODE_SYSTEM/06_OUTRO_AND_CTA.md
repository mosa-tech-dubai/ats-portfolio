# Outro and Call-to-Action

## Purpose

Close the episode with genuine value and open the door to community participation — without pivoting into a sales pitch for the guest.

## Structure

1. **Final Value (Episode Structure Section I)** — one highly useful takeaway question, e.g., "If you own a BMW, what's one thing you wish every owner knew?" This should feel like the natural end of the conversation, not a separate "wrap-up" segment.
2. **Community Question (Episode Structure Section J)** — a question posed directly to the audience designed to generate comments: "What's the worst problem you've ever had with your car?" / "What specialist should we visit next?" / "What should we ask this expert in a future live?" See `07_COMMUNITY/02_COMMENT_STRATEGY.md`.
3. **End card** — guest and business information (name, location, website, social, contact) presented as reference information, not narrated as a pitch. This is where indirect marketing becomes direct information, per `00_PROJECT_BIBLE/04_DECISION_PRINCIPLES.md` (the Ad Test): the *episode* stays editorial, the *end card* is allowed to be a directory listing.
4. **Subscribe/follow prompt** — brief, low-pressure, after the community question, never before it. Leading with "like and subscribe" undercuts the community-first framing.

## What Belongs in the Video Description (Not Narrated On-Camera)

- Business name, location, website, social media, contact details, relevant links.
- Timestamps for chapters.
- The community question restated as text, to prompt comments from people scrolling before watching.

## Constraints

- Never let the outro read as "go visit this business" in the narration — see `00_PROJECT_BIBLE/03_CORE_PHILOSOPHY.md`.
- Keep the end card visually consistent across episodes once `01_BRAND/06_VISUAL_IDENTITY.md` is finalized; until then, keep it simple and legible rather than improvising a new layout per episode.
