# Cold Open

## Purpose

30–60 seconds at the very start of the episode, before any branding or introduction, built from the strongest moment in the episode. Its only job is to create curiosity strong enough that the viewer stays through the intro.

## Examples

- "90% of people would replace this part even though it's perfectly fine."
- "A BMW came in with 15 faults. The actual problem was something nobody expected."

## What Makes a Strong Cold Open

- A specific, concrete claim or moment — not a vague teaser ("you won't believe what happens next").
- Pulled from an actual highlight logged during filming (see `04_HIGHLIGHT_SYSTEM.md`), never invented or exaggerated in the edit.
- Creates a genuine, answerable question in the viewer's mind that the episode will resolve.
- Works with sound off and minimal context, since it's often the first thing an algorithm-driven viewer sees.

## Process

1. Cold opens are selected **after** filming and initial review, once the strongest true highlight is known — not planned as a fixed line beforehand.
2. Pull the moment as close to verbatim as possible; light trimming for pacing is fine, but never reconstruct or paraphrase a claim to make it punchier than what was actually said.
3. Cut straight into the fast intro (`01_EPISODE_STRUCTURE.md`, Section B) immediately after — no branding sting before the cold open.

## Relationship to Short-Form

A strong cold open is very often independently reusable as a teaser clip — see `02_CONTENT_STRATEGY/05_CONTENT_REPURPOSING.md`.
