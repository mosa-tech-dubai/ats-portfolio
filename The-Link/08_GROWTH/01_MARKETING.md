# Marketing

## Marketing Is Indirect, By Design

The Link helps good businesses and specialists become discoverable without turning any episode into an advertisement. A viewer may arrive because "I have a BMW problem," then discover "this person specializes in exactly this problem" — the content itself creates the connection (`00_PROJECT_BIBLE/03_CORE_PHILOSOPHY.md`).

## What This Means in Practice

- Episode descriptions include useful, concrete guest/business information where appropriate: business name, location, website, social media, contact details, relevant links (`03_EPISODE_SYSTEM/06_OUTRO_AND_CTA.md`).
- The episode content itself stays editorial and content-first — never narrated as a pitch (`00_PROJECT_BIBLE/04_DECISION_PRINCIPLES.md`, the Ad Test).
- Marketing "for The Link" (growing the show's own audience) and "for the guest" (helping them become discoverable) are treated as the same activity — a well-made, honestly promoted episode does both simultaneously.

## Marketing The Link Itself

- Distribution follows `02_CONTENT_STRATEGY/04_PLATFORM_STRATEGY.md` — platform-adapted content, one consistent brand voice.
- Short-form content (`02_CONTENT_STRATEGY/05_CONTENT_REPURPOSING.md`) is the primary discovery engine; the full episode is the destination once someone is curious.
- Community mechanisms (`07_COMMUNITY/`) are themselves a growth channel — participatory audiences share and return more than passive ones.

## What The Link Does Not Do

- Paid advertising copy that oversells a guest's business beyond what the episode actually demonstrated.
- Deceptive titles/thumbnails that promise a claim the content doesn't support (fails the Value Test, `00_PROJECT_BIBLE/04_DECISION_PRINCIPLES.md`).
- Positioning as a "best of" ranking or review service (`01_BRAND/03_BRAND_POSITIONING.md`).
