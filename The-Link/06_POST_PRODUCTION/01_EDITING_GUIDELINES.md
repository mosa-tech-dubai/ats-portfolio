# Editing Guidelines (Long-Form)

## Edit Order

1. Assemble the full conversation in rough chronological/topical order, following `03_EPISODE_SYSTEM/01_EPISODE_STRUCTURE.md` as the map (sections can be reordered from the actual filming sequence if the story flows better that way).
2. Cut for story and pacing within each section before worrying about polish — a well-paced rough cut with temp audio beats the reverse.
3. Identify the strongest highlight for the cold open (`03_EPISODE_SYSTEM/05_COLD_OPEN.md`) once the full arc is known.
4. Finish the long-form cut before starting short-form extraction — see `02_SHORT_FORM_EDITING.md`.

## Pacing Principles

- Cut out redundant restatements and false starts, but preserve genuine hesitation, laughter, and reaction — these are what make the content feel human (`01_BRAND/04_TONE_OF_VOICE.md`).
- Never trim a guest's answer in a way that changes its meaning or makes a claim stronger/more definitive than what they actually said. Accuracy overrides pacing.
- Use B-roll and location footage to cover cuts and keep visual interest during long expertise explanations, rather than relying purely on jump cuts of the talking head.

## What Not to Do

- Don't add dramatic music stings or editing tricks that manufacture tension the actual footage doesn't support — see the Value Test and the ban on manufactured drama in `01_BRAND/04_TONE_OF_VOICE.md`.
- Don't cut around a guest's promotional language to "clean it up" into an ad — cut it out, or bring the conversation back to substance, rather than polishing a pitch.
- Don't bury the community question (Section J) in the very last seconds where viewers have already left — give it real room before the end card.
