# Tone of Voice

## The Tone, In Nine Words

Curious. Intelligent. Friendly. Slightly comedic. Human. Practical. Professional-not-corporate. Entertaining-not-fake. Informative-not-a-lecture.

## What Each Tension Means in Practice

**Curious, not interrogating.** Questions come from genuine interest, not a checklist to complete. See `03_EPISODE_SYSTEM/02_INTERVIEW_FRAMEWORK.md`.

**Intelligent, not academic.** We take the subject matter seriously and trust the audience to keep up, without turning explanations into jargon.

**Friendly, not sycophantic.** Warmth toward the guest, but not fawning or promotional language.

**Slightly comedic, never forced.** Humor comes from real moments and personalities. Never scripted jokes, never "comedy beats" inserted where nothing funny actually happened. See `03_EPISODE_SYSTEM/01_EPISODE_STRUCTURE.md`, Section H.

**Human, not polished-corporate.** Imperfections, real reactions, and genuine surprise are features, not editing mistakes to smooth over.

**Practical, not abstract.** Prioritize information a viewer could actually use — a warning sign to watch for, a mistake to avoid — over vague inspiration.

**Professional without being corporate.** Production quality and respect for the guest's time and expertise are non-negotiable; corporate jargon, brand-speak, and marketing language are not welcome in scripts, captions, or on-camera narration.

**Entertaining without becoming fake.** Retention and pacing matter, but never at the cost of manufacturing drama that didn't happen.

**Informative without becoming a lecture.** Deliver real value inside a story, not a monologue of facts.

## Voice Do's

- Ask short, open follow-ups: "How?" "Why?" "What happened next?" "Show us." "Wait, how did you figure that out?"
- Let silence and reaction breathe rather than filling every gap with narration.
- Use plain language before technical language, and explain technical terms the first time they appear.
- Write captions and descriptions the way a genuinely curious person would describe what they just saw.

## Voice Don'ts

- Don't use superlatives about the guest's business ("the best," "the #1 choice") — let the content demonstrate quality instead.
- Don't write copy that reads like a press release or a Google ad.
- Don't force a punchline onto a moment that wasn't naturally funny.
- Don't talk down to the audience or over-explain things a curious viewer already understands.

## Status

Applies to all on-camera narration, captions, video titles, descriptions, and social copy. Taglines are a separate, not-yet-developed asset — see `05_TAGLINES.md`.
