# Content Repurposing System

## The Rule

One long-form episode should generate multiple pieces of content. The production system is designed around content *extraction*, not just episode production — this is a structural requirement, not a nice-to-have applied after the fact.

## Potential Outputs From One Shoot

- Full episode (long-form, ~25–40 min — see `03_EPISODE_SYSTEM/01_EPISODE_STRUCTURE.md`)
- YouTube video (the full episode, optimized for YouTube specifically)
- Shorts / Reels / TikToks (multiple, per episode)
- Educational clips (a single piece of expert knowledge, isolated)
- Funny clips (a standalone humorous moment)
- Expert quote clips/graphics (a single strong statement, on camera or as a text graphic)
- Problem/solution clips (a specific issue and its fix, self-contained)
- "Did you know?" clips
- Myth vs. fact clips
- Audience-question clips (seeding or answering a community question)
- Teaser clips (pre-release, to build anticipation for the full episode)
- Behind-the-scenes content
- Still images (for social posts, thumbnails, articles)
- Thumbnails (multiple test variants — see `06_POST_PRODUCTION/04_THUMBNAILS.md`)
- Quote graphics (static, text-based)
- Future live-discussion topics (banked for `07_COMMUNITY/03_LIVE_FORMAT.md`)

## How Extraction Actually Happens

1. **Before filming:** the shot list and interview plan anticipate likely extraction moments (see `03_EPISODE_SYSTEM/04_HIGHLIGHT_SYSTEM.md` and `04_PILOT/04_PILOT_SHOT_LIST.md`).
2. **During filming:** the crew actively flags potential highlights in real time — educational, surprising, funny, emotional, practical, "you won't believe this," myth/fact, expensive-mistake moments — rather than waiting to discover them in the edit.
3. **In the edit:** the long-form cut is finished first; short-form clips are pulled from flagged highlight timestamps, not by re-watching the entire raw file from scratch every time.
4. **In scheduling:** outputs are staggered — teaser clips before release, full episode at release, remaining short-form and evergreen clips distributed afterward — rather than dumped all at once.

## One Shoot, Many Release Dates

Treating one shoot as a content mine means its outputs don't need to all publish in the same week. A single strong episode can responsibly supply short-form content across several weeks, which matters for maintaining publishing cadence between shoots.
