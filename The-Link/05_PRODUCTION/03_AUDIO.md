# Audio

Specific equipment, redundancy setup, and location-noise handling procedures have not yet been defined — this requires real-world testing, which the pilot shoot is designed to provide (see `04_PILOT/01_PILOT_OBJECTIVES.md`, which explicitly lists audio as a pilot test target).

## Principles to Follow Until a Full SOP Exists

- Workshop, garage, and studio locations are often loud and acoustically difficult (metal, concrete, running equipment). Plan for this rather than being surprised by it on shoot day.
- Guest audio (lavalier or equivalent close-mic'd source) should always be prioritized and backed up — a great interview with unusable audio is not usable content.
- Capture clean ambient/room tone at each location for editing flexibility.
- If a genuine demonstration involves loud tools or equipment, plan for it in advance (e.g., pausing narration during the loudest moment, or planning to let the moment play with natural sound and add clarifying captions in post) rather than fighting the noise live.
