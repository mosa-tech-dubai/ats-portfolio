# Location

## Why Location Matters Here

Episodes happen primarily at the guest's actual workplace — a garage, a workshop, a studio, a facility, a collection. This is a structural decision, not a preference: the location is part of the storytelling, and the audience should feel taken somewhere they normally couldn't access (`00_PROJECT_BIBLE/03_CORE_PHILOSOPHY.md`).

## What Makes a Good Location

- Visually active — tools, work in progress, objects with texture and history, not a bare or purely administrative space.
- Authentic to the guest's actual, everyday work — not staged or cleaned up beyond what's reasonable to ask.
- Practically filmable — enough space to move a small crew, adequate (or manageable) lighting and noise conditions.
- Safe for the crew and any planned demonstration footage.

## Location Etiquette

- The location belongs to the guest and, often, their customers or staff — treat it as a workplace being visited, not a set being taken over.
- Get explicit permission before filming any customer property (e.g., a customer's car in for repair) or identifiable staff/customers.
- Leave the space as it was found; don't rearrange a genuine workspace purely for aesthetics in ways that would misrepresent how the guest actually works.

## Location as Part of Vertical Expansion

Because the format is designed to move beyond automotive (`00_PROJECT_BIBLE/01_PROJECT_VISION.md`), location scouting criteria above are written to be vertical-agnostic on purpose — a watchmaker's bench and a transmission specialist's bay should be evaluated by the same standard.
